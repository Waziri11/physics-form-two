Why does the length increase with an increase of e.m.f.?

Theoretically, the e.m.f E1 of cell P balances the p.d. V1 on the wire because no current flows in G at the balance point N. As a result, E1=V1. Similarly, if cell E2 is the e.m.f of cell Q and V2 is the p.d. between A and M, E2=V2. As a result, E1E2=V1V2.

The p.d. V across any length, l, is directly proportional to l if the wire carries a constant current. As a result, E1E2=l1l2. Thus, the e.m.f of the two cells can be compared.

Example 2.12

The balance length of a potentiometer wire for a cell of e.m.f, E1=1.63 V is 85 cm. If the cell is replaced by another one of e.m.f, E2=1.07 V, calculate the new balance length.

Solution

Given

E1=1.63 V, l1=85 cm, E2=1.07 V

Then, the new length l2 is obtained from the formula:

E1E2=l1l2

Therefore,

l2=E2E1×l1
=1.07 V1.63 V×85 cm
=55.79 cm

The new balance length is 55.79 cm.
