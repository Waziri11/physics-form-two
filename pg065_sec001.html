Example 2.8

What is the resistance of a copper wire of length 20 m and a diameter of 0.080 cm? (Resistivity of copper, ρcu=1.68×10−8Ωm.)

Solution

Given that

l=20 m; for copper,
ρcu=1.68×10−8 Ωm
R=ρlA

But

A=πr2
=3.14×(4×10−4 m)2
=5.024×10−7 m2

Hence,

R=1.68×10−8 Ωm×20 m5.024×10−7 m2
=0.67\ \Omega

Therefore, the resistance of the wire is 0.67 Ω.

Example 2.9

A nichrome wire has a cross-sectional area of 4×10−8 m2 and a resistivity of 1.0×10−6 Ωm. If a resistor of resistance 11 Ω is to be made from this wire, calculate the length of the required wire.

Solution

Given that,

A=4×10−8 m2; ρ=1.0×10−6 Ωm; and R=11 Ω;

then,

l=ARρ
l=4×10−8 m2×11 Ω1.0×10−6 Ωm=0.44 m

Therefore, the length of the wire is 0.44 m.

Example 2.10

A constantan wire has a length of 45 cm, a diameter of 0.37 mm and a resistivity of 4.9×10−7 Ωm. (a) What is the resistance of the wire? (b) What will be the current flowing in the wire if it is connected to a 1.5 V cell?

Solution

(a)

ρ=1.0×10−6 Ωm; and l=0.45 m

But,

R=ρlA and A=πr2;

where,

r=0.37 mm2=0.185 mm
=1.85×10−4 m,

hence,

A=3.14×(1.85×10−4 m)2
=1.075×10−7 m2
R=4.9×10−7 Ωm×0.45 m1.075×10−7 m2=2.05 Ω

Resistance of the wire is 2.05 Ω.
