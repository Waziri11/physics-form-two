Magnification of a simple microscope

A simple microscope magnifies an object by increasing its angular size. The magnification produced by the simple microscope depends on the focal length of the lens. Lenses of short focal length give larger magnification than those with long focal length. In Figure 6.2(a), the object is at the near point, where it subtends an angle θobject to the eye. On the other hand, in Figure 6.2(b), the magnifier in front of the eye forms an image far away from the lens; the image subtends an angle θimage at the magnifier. The usefulness of the magnifier is given by the ratio of the angle subtended by the image (θimage) to the angle subtended by the object (θobject). This ratio is called the angular magnification, M, and is given by,

M=θimageθobject

The angular magnification should not be confused with the linear or lateral magnification, m, of a simple lens, which is the ratio of the image height, hi, to the object height, ho, given by,

m=hiho

Linear magnification is also given by the ratio of the image distance, v, to the object distance, u. That is:

m=vu

It follows that,

vu=hiho

and thus,

v=u×hiho

To find the value of angular magnification, let the angles subtended by the object and the image be very small, such that, sin⁡θobject=tan⁡θobject≈θobject and sin⁡θimage=tan⁡θimage≈θimage. Suppose the object in Figure 6.2 has a height, ho, and it is placed at the focal point, f=D=25 cm, where it subtends an angle θobject. If the angle θobject is very small, then,
