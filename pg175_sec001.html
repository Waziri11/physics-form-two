Refractive index of a material

In Activity 5.4, you observed that, for a given material or medium, the ratio sin⁡(i)sin⁡(r) is constant regardless of the size of the angle of incidence. This constant is known as the refractive index of the material (medium). It is a dimensionless (it has no unit) constant denoted by the Greek letter η. The refractive index of a material is a measure of the influence of the material on the speed of light as it passes through it. Therefore, the refractive index of a material is also obtained from the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum, c, to the speed of light in the material, v.

That is, η=cv.

To be specific, the ratio η=cv is known as the absolute refractive index of a material. If the ratio is obtained from the speed of light v1 in one material, to the speed of light v2 in the other material, then η=v1v2.

In this case, the ratio is called the relative refractive index.

Normally, the speed of light in air is considered as the speed of light in vacuum, c. Therefore, if light travels from air to water and from air to glass, it will have different speeds in water and glass because air, water and glass have different optical densities. Letting the speed of light in water be v1 and its speed in glass be v2 one can calculate the absolute refractive indices of water and glass as follows;

The absolute refractive index of water:
η1=cv1
This implies that, c=η1v1.
The absolute refractive index of glass:
η2=cv2
This implies that, c=η2v2.
