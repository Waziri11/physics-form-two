Angle of deviation

The angle of deviation is a measure of how much the incident ray has been deflected from its original direction by the prism. Consider a ray of light incident on a glass prism as shown in Figure 5.11.

where BAC^ = angle of prism or apex angle
i = angle of incidence
r = angle of refraction at the first surface
i2 = angle of incidence at the second surface
e = angle of emergence from the prism
D = angle of deviation. This is the angle between the initial incident ray direction and the final emergent ray direction.
Consider triangle ABC in Figure 5.11. The sum of internal angles is 180∘ that is,
ABC^+BAC^+ACB^=180∘
90∘−r+A+90∘−i2=180∘
180∘+A−r−i2=180∘
A=i2+r(1)
Now consider triangle MBC
MBC^+MCB^+BMC^=180∘
i−r+e−i2+180∘−D=180∘
i−r+e−i2=D(2)
r and e can be calculated by Snell’s law. Consider the first surface, by Snell’s law
η=sin⁡isin⁡r
In order to determine the angle of deviation D, we must consider very small angles, that is, sin⁡i≈i and sin⁡r≈r
Now
sin⁡isin⁡r=ir=η
Then,
i=ηr(3)
Consider the second surface, by Snell’s law
η=sin⁡esin⁡i2
This implies that,
ei2=η,and e=ηi2(4)
Substitute Equation (3) and (4) in Equation (2) we have, r(η−1)+i2(η−1)=D
ηr−r+ηi2−i2=D
Simplifying this equation, you get,
D=(r+i2)×(η−1)(5)
Combining Equations (1) and (5) we finally have, D=A×(η−1)
Therefore, the angle of deviation is given by D=A×(η−1).
